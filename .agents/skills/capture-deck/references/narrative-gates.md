# Narrative Gates

## The job of a Capture briefing

A briefing changes the learner's attention before the full consultation. It
does not compress every Chapter fact into slides. Decide what the learner must
notice, what tempting explanation should become less certain, and what question
should remain open when they continue into the Capture.

## Story spine worksheet

Write these before the slide list:

- **Learner arrives believing:** the plausible first instinct already present
  in the approved Chapter.
- **Concrete disturbance:** the Milo moment that makes that instinct
  insufficient.
- **Observation shift:** the detail the learner must learn to see separately
  from interpretation.
- **Evidence turn:** the approved claim that changes the decision.
- **Owner-level decision:** the safe action now supported.
- **Open question:** what the full Capture will explore rather than the deck
  pretending to settle.
- **Ending:** the sentence or image that returns to Milo and opens the Capture.

## Slide-spine format

Use this table before writing `.deck.mdx`:

| # | Assertion | Learning function | Visual intent | Claim/source |
| --- | --- | --- | --- | --- |
| 1 | Complete sentence | Orient / unsettle / distinguish / apply | Specific scene or relation | ID or `SCENE ONLY` |

An assertion should still make sense if read without the slide title. Avoid
headings such as `Safe rooms`, `Observation`, or `Evidence`; say what the slide
actually establishes.

## Educational rhythm

A useful default is:

1. Enter one concrete moment with Milo.
2. Surface the learner's likely first instinct.
3. Separate observation from the story placed on it.
4. Introduce only the evidence needed to revise the decision.
5. Let the learner make or inspect one small decision.
6. Return to the same Milo moment with a safer next action.
7. Continue into the full Capture with a question still worth pursuing.

This is not a fixed ten-slide template. Merge or split beats according to the
approved Chapter. The resulting deck must still have a beginning, a turn, and a
return rather than a sequence of fact cards.

## Human gates

For the first deck in a workflow:

1. Approve the content ledger and story spine.
2. Implement three anchor slides: opening scene, evidence turn, and decision.
3. Approve the visual language and density.
4. Complete the deck.
5. Review the rendered deck and QA record.

Do not infer approval from a successful build.

## Anti-slop questions

Before keeping a slide, ask:

- Does it move the learner from one state of judgement to another?
- Is the visual explaining a relationship, or merely filling space?
- Is the emotional note earned by a concrete event involving Milo?
- Does the headline and body say the same thing twice?
- Did this content earn its layout, or did the layout come first?
- Would cutting the slide damage the narrative spine?

If the last answer is no, cut it.
