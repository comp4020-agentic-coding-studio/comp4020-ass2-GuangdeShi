## 2. Expanded Chapter 4–12 skeleton (as presented)

**Chapter 4 — The Full Vocabulary** (Part II)
- Core question: What is Momo actually saying, once the easy signals (ears, tail, hiding) run out?
- Main content: multi-channel reading (ears/tail/whiskers/posture/vocalization together); context-dependence of the same signal; vocal repertoire beyond "meow"; scent communication basics (cheek-rubbing, flehmen response); a genuinely ambiguous multi-signal practice case.
- New capability: interpret combined signals in context, not recite a memorized single-cue list.
- Dependency: builds on Ch2's basic positive/negative body-language reading (`CH2-05`, `CH2-06`).
- Main evidence: `S3` (direct, strong); **SOURCE GAP** — deeper vocal/scent-communication content pending `S9`/`S10` textbook access; flagged as the most evidence-thin chapter in Part II.

**Chapter 5 — Needs, Not Naughtiness** (Part II)
- Core question: Why does "misbehavior" almost never mean what it looks like?
- Main content: the five-pillar environmental-needs framework (`S1`) as an ongoing system; scratching as territorial/identity marking with redirection strategy (`S26`); overgrooming via medical-first-then-behavioral elimination logic (`S27`), folded in rather than standalone; feeding as behavior (`S11`); the "never punish a need-driven behavior" principle applied across house-soiling/scratching/overgrooming.
- New capability: distinguish a need-driven behavior from a "problem" and design an environmental/behavioral response instead of assuming defiance.
- Dependency: builds on Ch1's environment setup and Ch4's multi-channel reading.
- Main evidence: `S1`, `S26`, `S27`, `S11` — strong cluster.

**Chapter 6 — Noticing What Changed** (Part II)
- Core question: How do I know something is actually different, not just an off day?
- Main content: turning Ch1's observation log into an active comparison tool; normal variation vs. meaningful pattern shift; a structured "compare to baseline" heuristic; worked cases (alarming-but-normal vs. minor-but-not); positioning this as the hinge skill for Chapters 9–11.
- New capability: compare current behavior against Momo's personal baseline and judge whether a change is meaningful — the capstone skill of Part II.
- Dependency: requires Ch1's observation-log habit (`CH1-09`) and Ch4/Ch5's interpretive vocabulary.
- Main evidence: primarily a method chapter reusing `S1`–`S4` as worked examples; **SOURCE GAP** for a dedicated behavioral-change-detection guideline (not strictly required — flagged as `SYNTHESIS` if written).

**Chapter 7 — Designing a Healthy Routine** (Part III)
- Core question: What does "healthy" look like day to day, not just at the annual checkup?
- Main content: body/muscle condition scoring as a repeatable skill (`S22`); feeding-as-behavior revisited from a routine-design angle (`S11`); vaccination as a risk-based vet conversation (`S12`); parasite prevention cadence (`S23`); household-variation note on cost/time constraints.
- New capability: assess body/muscle condition over time and hold an informed, questioning conversation with a vet about preventive care.
- Dependency: builds on Ch6's baseline-comparison skill, applied to physical condition.
- Main evidence: `S22`, `S11`, `S12`, `S23` — strong cluster.

**Chapter 8 — The Indoor/Outdoor Decision** (Part III)
- Core question: What does safety actually require for *this* cat, in *this* household?
- Main content: the 2024 position statement's core premise that no lifestyle is universally correct (`S24`); indoor-only environmental-needs requirements (linking to `S1`); controlled outdoor access (enclosures, harness/leash, building on Ch1/Ch2 skills); outdoor-only as a legitimate welfare choice in specific circumstances; universal microchip/ID baseline; household-variation note on space/budget/time.
- New capability: evaluate and design a lifestyle/access plan matched to a specific cat's temperament and household's real constraints, integrating welfare, risk, environment, temperament and household constraints as a trade-off — not a facts-only comparison.
- Dependency: reuses Ch1's carrier training and Ch2's consent-based handling in a new context.
- Main evidence: `S24` (single strong dedicated source), `S1` (secondary).

**Chapter 9 — The Signs Easy to Miss** (Part III)
- Core question: What is Momo hiding, and how would I know?
- Main content: why cats mask pain (evolved prey-species trait); applying Ch6's baseline-comparison method to subtle mobility/activity change; dental health as a commonly-missed area (`S14`, partial); preventive-visit cadence revisited; a subtle multi-week practice case.
- New capability: recognize subtle pain and dental signs owners commonly miss, and act on them.
- Dependency: directly reuses Ch6's baseline-comparison heuristic and Ch7's routine-assessment habit.
- Main evidence: **flagged as the weakest-evidenced chapter** — `S13` (pain guideline) access-blocked (secondary summary only); `S14` lacks a specific owner-facing warning-sign list in what had been read. Recommended resolving `S13` access (or an equivalent peer-reviewed source) before full authoring.

**Chapter 10 — The Aging Cat** (Part IV)
- Core question: What does "keeping her well" mean when wellness itself is changing?
- Main content: age-associated physical decline and common aging diseases (`S15`); the "budgets of care" framing (financial/time/emotional/physical) as the organizing idea, disclosed as `SYNTHESIS`; a senior-symptom checklist (`S20`) tempered by the budgets-of-care framing; mobility decline/DJD-prevalence hook (`S15` for substance, `S21` as opening device only); adapting nutrition/environment/vet cadence.
- New capability: recognize aging-related physical change and adapt routine/environment/vet cadence, scaled to the caregiver's real capacity.
- Dependency: builds on Ch6 (change detection), Ch7 (routine design), Ch9 (subtle-sign recognition).
- Main evidence: `S15`, `S20` (tempered), `S21` (hook only), `S2` — strong cluster.

**Chapter 11 — When Her Mind Changes** (Part IV)
- Core question: Is this just "getting old," or something I need to act on?
- Main content: cognitive dysfunction signs (`S25`); explicit reapplication of Ch3's medical-first reasoning to a new life stage, named as a callback; the rule-out sequence (arthritis, kidney disease, hyperthyroidism, hypertension, dental disease) before cognitive dysfunction is considered; owner-support adaptations; a practice case requiring the student to reapply Ch3's litter-box reasoning (`CH3-02`, `CH3-03`) plus this chapter's new context.
- New capability: apply the medical-first reasoning process learned in Ch3 to new behavior changes at a new life stage, recognizing cognitive-dysfunction signs without self-diagnosing.
- Dependency: explicitly reuses Ch3's reasoning method (deliberate, disclosed callback) plus Ch6 and Ch10.
- Main evidence: `S25` (strong, full text), `S15`/`S2` (corroborating).

**Chapter 12 — Deciding Together** (Part IV)
- Core question: How do we decide, together, what care looks like now?
- Main content: the "unit of care" concept (`S16`); the five-step hospice/palliative care-plan structure as a narrative framework, not a scored checklist (`S16`); reprising Ch10's budgets-of-care framing as final synthesis, disclosed as `SYNTHESIS` (`S15`+`S16`); relationship-centered caregiver–vet communication; closing reflection on the course's whole arc.
- New capability: participate in a caregiver-inclusive quality-of-life conversation and decision process, understood as a partnership, not a solo scored checklist — the course's capstone capability.
- Dependency: synthesizes Ch7 (routine/capacity), Ch10 (budgets of care), Ch11 (recognizing serious change).
- Main evidence: `S16` (strong, full text — QOL tool internals unverified, flagged), `S15` (corroborating).

## 3. Structural questions presented to the human

1. Overall Ch4–12 backbone as proposed (three-chapter arcs per Part, organized around accumulating capability).
2. Chapter 6 and Chapter 9 function substantially as method/reasoning chapters rather than pure new-fact chapters.
3. Chapter 3's medical-first reasoning is deliberately reused, not reinvented, in Chapter 11.
4. Chapter 9's evidentiary weakness (`S13` access-blocked) — proceed with the skeleton as-is and resolve sourcing before full authoring, or reshape now.
5. Overgrooming and predatory/play behavior folded into Chapter 5 rather than given standalone treatment.

## 4. Recommendation originally given

Conditional lock: approve the twelve-chapter structure and titles as the backbone, but treat Chapter 9 as provisionally locked pending evidence — sound position and capability claim, but full content authoring withheld until `S13` access is resolved or a substitute peer-reviewed pain-recognition source is found. All other eleven chapters judged to have a strong-enough evidence cluster to begin full content authoring once approved.
