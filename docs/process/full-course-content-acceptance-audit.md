# Full Course Content Acceptance Audit

Date: 2026-09-18
Scope: Chapters 01–12, learner-facing Capture prose, evidence notes, guided
readings, practice, Care Plans, and formative Chapter Checks.

## Decision

**ACCEPTED.** The implemented course forms one progressive twelve-week learning
experience rather than twelve interchangeable cat-care topics. No chapter needs
to be removed, reordered, or rewritten, and no curriculum-backbone change was
made during this audit.

The learner progression is visible in the work students perform:

```text
prepare and observe
  -> read changing signals
  -> interpret needs and meaningful change
  -> make proportionate owner-level decisions
  -> adapt care across a lifetime
  -> participate in shared professional decisions
```

## Audit method

The audit compared the implemented course against `CLAUDE.md`,
`docs/CURRICULUM.md`, `docs/VOICE.md`, `docs/LESSON_SYSTEM.md`, every
`docs/chapters/chapter-XX.md` file, and the Claim Register in
`docs/SOURCES.md`. It inspected student-facing prose rather than relying only
on passing tests. It also checked claim/source coverage, DIRECT/SYNTHESIS
labelling, workload arithmetic, activity depth, Chapter Check design, medical
scope, continuity, repeated language, and cross-chapter dependency.

## Evidence and medical-scope findings

- Every chapter-specific Claim ID planned in the authoritative chapter files
  appears in the implemented Capture or its Chapter Check. No approved claim
  disappeared during web production.
- Learning-thread and evidence-note support labels do not present a registered
  SYNTHESIS claim as DIRECT evidence.
- Chapter 2's S3 note discussed cooperative care but omitted `CH2-08` from its
  displayed claim list. The audit corrected that traceability omission; the
  teaching claim and source did not change.
- The course consistently keeps the learner at observe, record, compare,
  adjust safe environmental factors, and seek professional help. Diagnosis,
  prescribing, pain medication, supplement choice, and clinical rule-out stay
  with veterinary professionals.
- Known source limitations remain visible and are handled conservatively. The
  student-facing course does not quote an unverified cat-specific parasite
  cadence from S23, invent a fixed senior visit interval from partially
  accessed S15, reproduce an unverified quality-of-life score from S16, or use
  the excluded S21 prevalence statistic.

## Progression and continuity findings

The key dependencies are genuine teaching dependencies, not decorative name
checks: Chapter 1's log enables Chapters 3 and 6; Chapter 4's combined reading
enables Chapter 5; Chapter 6's method is applied rather than retaught in
Chapters 7, 9, 10, and 11; Chapter 8's lifestyle decision is reopened in
Chapter 10; Chapter 3's medical-first reasoning returns under real ambiguity in
Chapter 11; and Chapters 1, 6, 7, 10, and 11 converge in the Chapter 12
capstone.

A fresh TF-IDF comparison of the Chapter 02–12 teaching prose found no
high-similarity pair. The highest pair was Chapters 10 and 12 at 0.320, which
is explained by the deliberate progression from budgets of care to
caregiver-inclusive quality-of-life decisions. No repeated paragraph or
duplicated chapter capability was found.

## Assessment and voice findings

Each Chapter Check contains four core-concept questions, two scenarios, and
one synthesis question. Distractors represent recurring misconceptions such
as forced reassurance, single-cue interpretation, indefinite waiting,
premature diagnosis, universal lifestyle rules, and fixed care checklists.
Every option supplies explanatory feedback, and answer positions remain
balanced by the tested ordering system.

The student-facing prose follows the approved field-notebook voice and contains
none of the banned stock vocabulary listed in `docs/VOICE.md`. The reusable
nine-phase rhythm is consistent, but chapter scenes, reasoning methods,
practice tasks, and care decisions remain domain-specific. Repeated feedback
phrasing such as “This is the best answer” is a stable assessment convention,
not repeated teaching content.

## Documentation reconciliation

The curriculum and chapter plans still displayed drafting-era `TBD`,
`PROPOSED`, and “pending audit” status language even though all twelve Captures
had passed Human Review. This audit updated current status declarations and the
final learner capability without altering chapter order, questions, claims,
scenarios, or teaching content. Historical proposal labels inside the planning
records remain as process evidence and are explicitly identified as such.

## Remaining non-blocking limitations

The source limitations above remain honest constraints, not unfinished course
content. Any future addition of exact parasite cadence, a fixed senior-visit
interval, detailed hospice steps, or a quality-of-life scoring tool would
require new source verification and Human Review. None is required for the
current owner-facing learning promise.
