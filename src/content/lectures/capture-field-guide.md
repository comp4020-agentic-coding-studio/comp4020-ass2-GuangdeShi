This field guide sits beside the twelve dated Captures. It is not another teaching week. Return to
it whenever you want to check where you are in the course's recurring learning rhythm.

## The recurring movement

A Capture begins with a moment involving Milo and ends with a decision you can explain and revise.
The labels stay familiar while the problem, evidence and owner-level action change across her life.

### Scene

Meet Milo in one concrete moment. Stay with what the room, routine and body language make visible.

### Observation

Record what you can see, hear, count or compare before naming a cause.

### Judgement

Make a first decision while uncertainty is still present. This gives you something real to revisit.

### Evidence

Use the explanation and source note to test that first judgement, including the limits of what an
owner can safely conclude.

### Practice

Apply the same reasoning to a different household or a changed condition instead of repeating
Milo's answer.

### Reflection

Return to your opening note and identify what changed in your interpretation.

### Care Plan

Choose practical owner-level actions, what to observe next, and when professional help is needed.

### Chapter Check

Use the formative questions and option-specific feedback to revise your reasoning. They do not add
to the formal assessment percentage.

## How to use the field notebook

Save concrete observations and reasoning as you work. These notes stay on your device and are not
submitted automatically. The formal assignments ask you to select, revise and explain the evidence
that genuinely changed your judgement.
