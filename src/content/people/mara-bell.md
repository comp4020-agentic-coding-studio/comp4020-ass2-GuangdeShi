> **Fictional course-world profile.** Mara Bell is not an ANU staff member, a
> veterinarian or an emergency contact.

## Teaching approach

Mara's consultations begin with a small correction: slow the story down until its observable parts
can be seen. She will ask what changed, compared with which baseline, over what period, and what
would make the current judgement unsafe. The aim is not to make every learner sound clinically
certain. It is to make their uncertainty specific enough to act on responsibly.

Feedback follows the course rhythm. First it identifies what the learner noticed well. Then it tests
the interpretation against another plausible reading. Finally it asks whether the recommended
owner action has a clear endpoint and preserves the boundary between home care and professional
assessment.

## Contact rhythm

Post general course and assessment questions in the shared course space so the answer can help
everyone. Use a private course message for extensions, access needs or circumstances you do not
want to discuss publicly. A weekly online consultation hour is reserved for bringing one field note,
case decision or draft care plan into a short conversation.

Questions about a real cat's health belong with a veterinarian. An urgent concern should go to an
appropriate veterinary or emergency service rather than waiting for a course reply.
