## Purpose

Before you can interpret change, you need something sturdier than memory. This task tests whether
you can create a record another caregiver could read without having to guess what *clingy*, *odd*,
or *fine* meant that day.

## The brief

> Make one week of ordinary life precise enough to become a trustworthy baseline.

Use the supplied observation set or a tutor-approved, non-invasive record from a cat you already
live with. Cat ownership is not required, and no animal should be disturbed for the assignment.

## What you submit

Submit the notebook, the baseline portrait, and a commentary that revisits one interpretation you
were initially tempted to make. Identify the observed detail that supported it, the evidence that
complicated it, and the more careful wording you would now use.

## Basic expectations

Strong work is selective rather than exhaustive. It notices repeatable patterns, records context,
and says when the evidence is incomplete. It does not turn a short observation window into a
personality verdict or a home diagnosis.

## Timing

Due at the end of Part I, after Capture 04. Feedback arrives before the larger consultation task so
you can repair the quality of your baseline before it carries more analytical weight.
